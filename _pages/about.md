I am Katherine, a Ph.D. student at the University of Washington Paul G. Allen School of Computer Science & Engineering. I am broadly interested in Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) and health. I approach research through a decolonial lens, questioning whose values and ways of knowing become ingrained in the systems we design, and who gets left out. Through my research, I hope to collaborate with underserved communities to build systems that have the potential to address health disparities.

In the health space, I've collaborated on projects exploring how people use generative AI to make sense of their own personal health data and how people would want to record and pass down menopause experiences to future generations. My previous work examined how Latin American users adapted and adopted imported digital technologies designed in the United States. 

I completed my undergraduate studies at Wake Forest University, earning a B.S. in Computer Science with a minor in Biology. There I worked with Professor [Errin W. Fulp](https://fulp.cs.wfu.edu/index.html) on developing runtime execution profiles for predicting process behavior using machine learning techniques such as SVMs and clustering. I also worked with Professor [Mary Jane Carmichael](https://www.hollins.edu/directory/mary-jane-carmichael/) to study greenhouse gas fluxes in wetlands that are often overlooked in global carbon budgets.

I have experience working as a software engineer for Red Ventures and Comcast, where I am currently a cybersecurity software engineer. I truly enjoy building systems as much as I like studying them. Outside of research and engineering, I love languages! I speak English, Spanish (Native), and Portuguese (Advanced), and most of my Ph.D. research has been conducted in Spanish.
